Fun book with interesting digressions. However, the book is very short. Too short. Obviously there will be more installments as this is part one, but it does not make for a substative volume. basically each section is a essay that pulls from two sources. It makes for good reading but I think it leans a bit too high-level leaving it feeling like a series of short book reports. 

I liked the sections on precision manufacturing, rust, and military sustainment. Also the digression on the repair and modification culture that sprung up around the model-T's was really inspiring. It's so sad what we have lost. However, I am now more inspired to take on my own repairs and going forward I'm going to make sure I prioritize repairability for big purchases.

The gimick of this book is that it was written online and in public and some of the comments on the work are published in the margins. They didn't include that many so it's not distracting, but when they are there they don't feel like they add anything. 

Lastly, the images in the book are very low quality. For a sticker price of 40 bucks (I it got a good bit cheaper) you would think they could print in color. Some of the images are detail shots of rust but printed as just a block of grey. Huge mistep. 

Good writing, but too short, cheap printing, lame comment gimick. Verdict? It's a cash grab and I am looking forward to the next volume anyway!
