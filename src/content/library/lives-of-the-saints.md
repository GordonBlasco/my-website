Loved it. Usually not a fan of "decadance" literature as it can devolve into cataloging binges which is really uninteresting. However, Lives of the Saints is nothing like that. This book was fun and then devastating. Lemann is a master of the craft. Simple as.

The book sports a very fun southern vocabulary: maudlin, lugubrious, paltry, sultry, steamy, ardor, lurid, sordor, mirth, histrionic, solicitude, debutantes, voluptuous, sumptuous, scintillating, foppish. Honestly it would all be too much in any other book, but it feels very natural here. On top of the decadent vocabulary, there are these repeated sayings, both in the dialog and narration, that give the book a homeric quality.

Pulling something off that is so stylistic is actually crazy. 

It gave me the vapors/10
